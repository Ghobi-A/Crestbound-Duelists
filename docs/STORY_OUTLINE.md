# Story Outline

Four-act structure. This document tracks current story canon and replaces the older Aren/Veyrhold version. See `LORE_BIBLE.md` for world rules and characters.

## Act I — The Crest at Greymere

Locations: **Greymere Town → the Hollow Court → Civara**.

### Greymere baseline

Kai begins as a reserved, observant and independent young man who is comfortable in Greymere rather than desperate to escape it. His closest relationships are Joey, Lena, Danfor, Elder Silas and Gell. His early naivety is not simple gullibility: Kai understands individuals reasonably well but has little experience of institutions, geopolitics or systems whose outcomes cannot be reduced to one person's intentions.

Small abnormalities begin around Greymere. Silas reacts with genuine fear. **Warden Almyra** arrives with a small Avelan specialist unit under the legitimate cover of a geological inspection, but Avelan has actually detected abnormal Crest resonance beneath the town. Almyra and Silas recognise one another, revealing an unexplained prior connection.

### The Hollow Court incident

The **Hollow Court** is an ancient pre-Avelan Warden site connected to the Veil. A dormant Crest has remained beneath Greymere for centuries and has rejected previous attempts at bonding.

A disturbance elsewhere in the Veil network causes the site to react. This will eventually connect to **Cassian Clave's** attempts to locate and understand important Crest sites, but Cassian is not specifically searching for Kai and does not yet know what Greymere will produce.

A local structural failure opens access to the Court. Almyra's sanctioned specialists enter. A bonding attempt fails and activates ancient systems, sealing passages and producing Riven manifestations.

Joey becomes trapped below during the resulting emergency. Kai enters primarily to retrieve his friend, not because of destiny or heroic ambition. Silas warns him: **if he hears something calling him, do not answer it.**

Kai reaches Joey and the dormant Crest. During the collapse the Crest activates and Kai experiences an impossible memory that is not his own: a fragment from the ancient world associated with Eidros and the Veil. The Crest bonds to Kai without a conventional conscious acceptance.

The bond stabilises part of the Court but nearly kills him. Almyra's specialists discover that the Crest has integrated unusually deeply; removing it would kill Kai.

Silas recognises something about the bonded Crest but refuses to explain everything he knows.

### Why Almyra already knows Kai

Avelan possesses records from an earlier quiet survey of Greymere residents around the dormant site. Kai produced contradictory/inconclusive Crest-resonance results as a child. Almyra has seen his name in those records, but she did **not** know he would bond with the Hollow Court Crest. Kai is not currently defined as a secret descendant or predetermined chosen one.

### The non-choice

An unregistered civilian is now permanently bonded to an unknown high-order Crest at a politically sensitive pre-Accord site. Avelan offers two legal paths:

1. **Permanent protective custody** — restricted movement while the state studies and manages the Crest.
2. **Duelist classification** — enter the internationally recognised sanctioned Duelist system, allowing training, monitored travel and an eventual route to independent status.

Kai chooses the Duelist path because it preserves the greatest degree of autonomy. He does **not** initially enter because he dreams of becoming the world's greatest Duelist.

Greymere survives the opening. It is damaged and changed, but remains a place Kai can return to later.

## Act I, Part II — Civara

Kai travels to **Civara**, a major Avelan administrative and Duelist city. Its dense Renaissance civic architecture, bureaucracy, foreign presence and normalised Crest activity contrast sharply with rural Greymere.

In Greymere, Almyra appeared to be *the authority*. Civara reveals that she is one actor inside a much larger system. Departments dispute jurisdiction over Kai; researchers request access; military interests want his potential assessed; Accord interests care about the Hollow Court; and Almyra spends political capital preventing Kai from being consumed by those competing interests.

This begins Kai's central early lesson: a person can sincerely want to protect him while still participating in an institution capable of exploiting him.

### Kai's class selection

Kai's class is a **player choice**, not canonically Neutral and not determined by his personality.

During Avelan classification, his unusual Crest responds to all six recognised resonance patterns when it should naturally settle into one. The assessment is repeated and produces the same result. The player then chooses which resonance Kai deliberately stabilises.

| Class | Crest | Colour | Principle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warrior | Crimson | Crimson red | **Pressure** — advance, break, overpower |
| Guardian | Azure | Deep azure blue | **Endurance** — occupy space, protect, withstand |
| Mage | Ember | Burnt orange / amber | **Projection** — channel power outward |
| Sorcerer | Eclipse | Violet-black | **Manipulation** — alter rules, statuses and opponents |
| Assassin | Glass | Ice-cyan / silver-white | **Precision** — speed, openings, decisive strikes |
| Neutral | Verdant | Deep green | **Adaptation** — flexibility without specialisation |

Once stabilised, the selected class becomes Kai's build identity rather than allowing unrestricted class swapping.

### Liora Sen

**Liora Sen** is present as Prudentian Crest expertise. She witnesses all six resonance channels respond to Kai and becomes fascinated by the anomalous result. Her initial interest is explicitly the Crest and its behaviour; her relationship with Kai becomes genuine later.

Liora is intensely curious and intellectually driven. Her eventual flaw is not the same as Almyra's paternalism: Liora can allow the pursuit of understanding to become more important than the person being studied.

### Rin

Kai encounters **Rin** around his first formal combat assessment rather than through an immediate theatrical rivalry challenge.

Rin is already technically accomplished. Kai, by contrast, has instinct and unusual Crest compatibility but lacks formal Duelist fundamentals, terminology and efficient Crest control. Rin concludes that Greymere gave Kai an extraordinary opportunity but did not make him a Duelist.

Rin's early criticism is essentially correct. Kai does not become defensive; he accepts valid criticism and trains.

Their first meaningful duel should be a **clean Rin victory**. Kai does something clever enough to force Rin to adjust, but Rin is substantially better at this stage. Kai treats the defeat as another hurdle rather than a status injury.

## The Duelist Trial

The **Duelist Trial** is not merely a tournament. It is the practical certification mechanism created under the **Veiled Accord** for legal Crest-bearing operatives.

The Accord restricts uncontrolled Crest use, but states still require sanctioned Duelists to respond to Riven outbreaks, unstable Crest sites, illegal Duelists, Bonded Entities, Veil disturbances and international Crest incidents. Passing recognised certification provides a legal route for Crest use and mobility.

In practice, states have turned the Trial into a mixture of **licensing, sport, military recruitment, research, diplomacy and propaganda**.

Kai initially understands the public certification function far better than the political machinery underneath it.

### Phase I — Qualification

Individual tests covering combat fundamentals, Crest control, Bonded Entity compatibility and Riven-response scenarios.

Kai struggles with formal skills other candidates consider elementary but performs unusually well when a test stops having an expected solution and requires improvisation.

### Phase II — Cohort

Candidates are assigned to temporary teams. Class composition, cooperation and tactical adaptation become central. Kai cannot progress solely through individual combat ability.

### Phase III — Trial proper

Competitive encounters and practical operations between surviving candidates/teams. Assessment is cumulative rather than a simple single-elimination bracket, allowing major characters to lose meaningful fights without mechanically ending their stories.

### First uncontrolled Trial incident

During what should be a controlled Riven exercise, an Entity manifests far beyond its expected classification. The exercise becomes a genuine containment emergency.

Kai's Crest reacts before he consciously invokes it. For a moment the Riven Entity appears to **recognise** him and says only:

> **“Still?”**

It then attacks.

Kai, Rin and other candidates must fight for real. Rin sees that Kai becomes considerably more effective when the prescribed solution collapses and improvisation becomes necessary. Kai sees that Rin's confidence is backed by responsibility: under real danger Rin prioritises protecting and coordinating other candidates over personal glory.

This is the beginning of genuine mutual respect.

## Crest memories

Kai intermittently experiences fragments that appear to belong to the Crest rather than himself: an unknown battlefield, the Hollow Court before its ruin, figures from Eidros's era, and incomplete moments surrounding the Veil.

The historical events remain canonically the same regardless of class, but **class choice changes Kai's perceptual emphasis**:

- Warrior notices conflict, force and physical action.
- Guardian notices what was being defended or contained.
- Mage perceives structures and flows of power.
- Sorcerer notices distortions, corruption and abnormalities beneath events.
- Assassin notices critical openings, failures and decisive moments.
- Neutral notices relationships and connections between multiple elements.

This gives class choice narrative texture without creating six incompatible versions of history.

Silas eventually asks Kai whether he has dreamed since the Court. Kai has, but initially withholds that fact, establishing how his self-contained nature can become a liability.

---

## Cassian Clave — first contact

Cassian should not first appear framed as the obvious final antagonist.

Following the uncontrolled Riven incident, Kai encounters an intelligent, composed stranger away from the investigators. **Cassian Clave** recognises him as the Greymere Duelist, while Kai does not initially know Cassian's identity.

Their first interaction is an ordinary conversation. Cassian may even give Kai genuinely useful advice. His interest is ideological and investigative rather than theatrical villainy.

The purpose is to establish, long before the central conflict is revealed, that Cassian is rational, personable and capable of making arguments Kai understands.

Later, Almyra's reaction to learning Kai spoke with him reveals that the encounter was significant.

## Act II — The Quiet Arms Race

Locations include **Belgrave**, **Veritas**, **Solharbor** and eventually other territories.

The Trial opens Kai onto the international reality of Crests: sanctioned versus Unbound Duelists, forbidden research, Riven Crests, competing national programmes and the quiet arms race hidden beneath the Veiled Accord.

Kai and Rin progress from competitors to reluctant allies and eventually close but argumentative companions. Kai's Greymere worldview is challenged not because he is foolish, but because understanding individual motives proves insufficient for understanding systems.

---

## Act III — Fracture

Open political escalation. Betrayal and major consequences test Kai's willingness to give second chances and his habit of privately deciding where boundaries lie. Cassian is revealed as a principal hand accelerating the crisis.

Cassian's broad plan remains:

1. Increase tension.
2. Force states to mobilise hidden Crest resources.
3. Use that mobilisation to locate the world's important Crests and Veil sites.
4. Gather or expose them.
5. Destroy the Crest system before humanity can weaponise it beyond recovery.

Cassian's diagnosis is partly correct; his error is granting himself the authority to impose the solution on everyone.

---

## Act IV — The First Warden

The truth about **Eidros, the First Warden**, and **the Veil** is revealed: Crests are not merely weapons or fragments of power but part of an ancient containment system created from the remains/power of the protector whose death shaped the modern world.

Former enemies are forced into cooperation as Cassian's actions destabilise what he intended to dismantle safely.

The central ideological conflict is not optimism versus nihilism:

- **Kai** begins too zoomed-in, judging people and problems primarily case-by-case.
- **Cassian** becomes too zoomed-out, reducing individuals to systemic incentives and recurring historical patterns.

Kai must learn that individual compassion and judgement are necessary but not sufficient for governing systems. Cassian must confront the fact that recognising systemic danger does not grant him the right to decide humanity's future unilaterally.

The ending should remain optimistic but consequence-heavy: the truth about Crests and the Veil spreads, existing monopolies fracture, and no single state or individual receives uncontested control of Crest knowledge.

## Kai's character arc

Kai begins **reserved, observant, independent, pragmatic and quietly compassionate**. He is socially capable rather than shy, accepts justified criticism, is competitive without strong status insecurity, and values autonomy intensely.

His early naivety is primarily **systemic**. Greymere taught him to understand problems through individual people and circumstances. The wider world forces him to understand institutions, incentives, collective action and consequences that exist even when no participant is individually malicious.

His major flaws include poorly communicated boundaries, excessive self-containment, willingness to repeatedly rescue people from self-created problems, and rare but potentially disproportionate aggression once an internal threshold is crossed.

The original thematic principle remains useful but no longer defines his entire personality:

> **Compassion without judgement is naivety. Judgement without compassion is cruelty.**

Kai's growth is toward judgement **and** compassion operating at both interpersonal and systemic scales without surrendering his independence or becoming Cassian.

---

## Core relationship direction

- **Kai / Joey:** oldest friendship; impulsive Joey versus observant Kai; a rescue-dependency loop eventually needs consequences.
- **Kai / Rin:** competitors → respect → reluctant allies → close, teasing, argumentative friendship; neither requires the other's failure for self-worth.
- **Kai / Almyra:** autonomy versus paternalistic responsibility. Almyra can genuinely care about Kai while deceiving him for what she believes are protective reasons.
- **Kai / Liora:** genuine intellectual/personal bond complicated by Liora's capacity to prioritise knowledge over consent.
- **Kai / Silas:** curiosity and selective truth; Silas knows old Crest history but does not speak in artificial riddles.
- **Lena / Danfor:** practical compassion versus procedural consistency; frequent disagreement but excellent cooperation in genuine emergencies.
- **Cassian / Kai:** systemic reasoning versus individual judgement, with each eventually forced to confront the weaknesses of his own scale of analysis.

## Prototype scope

The current playable prototype may remain substantially more compressed than this full narrative. Implementation should not be assumed to contain every story beat above until the corresponding production pass is scheduled.

## Player collection direction

Crests/builds, Crest fragments for awakening/lore, Bonded Entities earned through bonds/trials/sites/story rather than capture mechanics, and a small number of recruitable Duelists. First-game scope remains a focused party rather than hundreds of collectible characters.

## Death rules

Major characters may die in the story. There is no gameplay permadeath for the core party in the first game. A dead Duelist's Crest usually goes dormant and their Entity usually vanishes; rare Echoes may exist later.
