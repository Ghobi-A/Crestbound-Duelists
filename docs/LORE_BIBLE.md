# Lore Bible

Working canon for Crestbound Duelists. Implementation must stay
compatible with this document; change it deliberately and note why.

## Tone

Dark fantasy with a slightly political backdrop and Final
Fantasy-level maturity. An initially peaceful world with mounting
tension; tragedy without nihilism; an ultimately optimistic ending.
Friendship, humour, discovery, and ordinary life exist alongside the
darker material.

**Thematic statement:** *Power does not corrupt everyone in the same
way. It reveals and amplifies what people are already willing to
become.*

## The world

Several medieval-fantasy kingdoms — technologically advanced for the
genre (sophisticated mechanisms, Crest-powered relic technology, early
industry in wealthy regions) — are bound by a fragile agreement, the
**Veiled Accord**, restricting Crest possession, research, and
military use. Publicly the Accord prevents another Crest catastrophe.
Privately every major government violates it, because none can afford
to let rivals hold more Crest power: a deterrence arms race.

### The four powers

| Power | Identity | Key location |
|-------|----------|--------------|
| **Kingdom of Avelaine** | Stable, cautious, bureaucratic; publicly anti-Crest, privately keeps sanctioned Duelists and hidden archives. Subtle hypocrisy. | Greymere, Veyrhold, the Hollow Court |
| **Dominion of Dravenn** | Disciplined, expansionist, invested in military Crest research. Argument: restraint alone will not protect us. | Bellgrave (fortified frontier city) |
| **Republic of Caelune** | Scholarly, mercantile; publicly values knowledge, privately controls forbidden Crest history. | Caer Lumen (the great archive city) |
| **Crown of Solmere** | Wealthy maritime trader, flexible neutral intermediary, profits from the Crest economy. | Solharbor (trade, espionage, illegal Crest markets) |

Also: **Evershade**, an ancient settlement preserving pre-kingdom
Crest traditions (important later); **the First Vault**, a late-game
site tied to the fragmentation of the dead god (not in the prototype).

### Greymere and the Hollow Court

Greymere: the protagonist's peaceful, slightly isolated border town in
Avelaine — ordinary life first, conflict later. Beneath/near it lies
the **Hollow Court**: publicly an empty ruin, actually a sealed Crest
site holding dormant divine fragments and forbidden records. The
opening incident happens here.

## Crests

Long ago the world was protected by **Eidros, the First Warden**.
Public religion says Eidros disappeared after averting an ancient
catastrophe. The forbidden truth: **Eidros died**, and its fractured
divine power became the Crests. Each Crest carries a fragment of
power, an aspect of Eidros, incomplete ancient memory, and an innate
nature (protection, ambition, vengeance, curiosity, control,
sacrifice, destruction, adaptation…). A Crest is not conscious, but it
amplifies compatible traits in its bearer.

- **Acceptance/rejection:** a Crest must resonate with its bearer.
  Morality is irrelevant; compatibility is everything. Soldiers and
  elites can be rejected; ordinary people accepted. Forced bonding can
  injure, break, or kill. Governments fear this — armies can be
  controlled, but not who a fragment of a dead god chooses.
- **Resonance:** the growing connection between Duelist and Crest.
  Moderate levels grant power, abilities, stronger Entities, and
  awakenings. Dangerous levels let the Crest's nature push the bearer
  (protection → inability to abandon anyone; courage → recklessness;
  responsibility → possessiveness) until they cannot distinguish their
  own wants from the Crest's. Extremes damage body, magic, mind.
- **Awakening:** discovering a previously inaccessible expression of
  the Crest — tied to experience, personality, pressure, and tactics,
  not levels.
- **Riven Crests:** fragments distorted by centuries of war, traumatic
  Resonance, ambition, and failed experiments. Not inherently evil,
  but they push bearers toward obsession, paranoia, domination.
  Initially treated as uncertain/forbidden knowledge.
- Rarely, an exceptional individual binds more than one Crest.
- Deep secret (Act IV): Crests also maintain **the Veil**, a system
  Eidros created to contain an ancient catastrophe. Destroying them
  all would end the arms race — and the Veil.

## Bonded Entities

Manifestations produced by Duelist + Crest + Resonance — beasts,
spirits, masks, ancestral figures, living weapons, relic machines,
abstract forms. The same Crest bonded to a different person can
produce a different Entity. Intelligence varies (instinctive to
articulate); relationships vary (friendship, dependency, conflict,
manipulation). At high Resonance an Entity may act against the
Duelist's conscious wishes, claiming to express their deeper nature.
Whether Entities are independent beings, divine memory, reflections of
the bearer, or all three is deliberately ambiguous.

When a Duelist dies their Crest usually goes dormant and the Entity
disappears. Rare survivors are called **Echoes**; nobody understands
what they are. (Not in the prototype.)

## Duelists

Crest-bearers capable of combat. Legal recognition is political:
**Sanctioned Duelists** serve governments, militaries, houses, and
institutions; **Unbound Duelists** are unregistered — treated as
criminals, actually mercenaries, rebels, protectors, smugglers,
dissidents, or simply people the institutions rejected.

**Formal duels** — culturally significant, publicly discouraged — are
used to settle Crest ownership, political conflicts, access to ancient
sites, succession, and rivalries. As order deteriorates, duelling
resurges (the narrative justification for frequent non-lethal tactical
battles).

## Classes (lore)

Classes are innate expressions of personality, instinct, affinity, and
Crest resonance — not professions. Change is possible after a profound
identity shift, but rare.

- **Warrior** — direct action, conviction, force, confrontation
- **Guardian** — protection, endurance, responsibility, sacrifice
- **Mage** — understanding, structured control, broad technical toolkit
- **Sorcerer** — instinctive force, aggression, curses; more raw power, less utility than Mages
- **Assassin** — decisiveness, precision, speed, exploiting openings (not necessarily a murderer)
- **Neutral** — extremely rare; adaptive, unpredictable, politically valuable, potentially dangerous
